# Fans vs Favorites — Favorites continuity bible

This is the episode-writing authority for the twelve Favorites. Source canon defines the person; the simulator archive defines what happened in this franchise. Interpretation is labeled and never overwrites either. Thom uses Tom’s canon identity plus only Thom’s Seasons 11 and 13 archive.

## Bowie

### Canon baseline

Bowie is openly competitive, socially alert, and comfortable being the most visibly confident person in a room. He enjoys drama as both entertainment and information, notices who needs reassurance, and can build sincere friendships even while planning around them. He prefers clean strategic authorship: identify the dangerous player, assemble the numbers, and own the decision in confessional. His affection for people such as Raj reveals a real boundary beneath the ambition; he can postpone or soften a move when the human cost becomes personal. Under pressure he gets sharper and more controlling, but not randomly cruel. He respects opponents who can answer him move for move and hates being treated as strategically naive.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 9: 1st (Winner).** Style: Advantage hoarder, social spider. 5 Votes To Win / Adv. Master
  - Ep5: Navigates idol chaos and revote to decapitate Hicks without exposure.
  - Ep8: Plays Inheritance to absorb Knowledge is Power and an Extra Vote.
  - Ep11: Uses Knowledge is Power to take Julia’s idol.
  - Ep12: Breaks Unbreakable bond to blindside Priya.
  - Ep13: Wins immunity; pairs Extra Vote with Chase’s Vote Stopper to bury Julia.
  - Ep15: Beats Damien at fire to secure Final 3.
  - Alliances: Chase, Damien, Axel, Priya, Millie.
  - Rivalries: Julia, Emmah.
- **Season 10: 2nd (Finalist).** Style: Ruthless architectural tactician. Bowie plays the season's sharpest strategic game but loses the final when the course demands more than control.
  - Episode 1: Finds the Champions Hidden Immunity Idol and forms The Movement.
  - Episode 3: Publicly points The Movement at Courtney while privately voting out Mickey.
  - Episode 4: Confronts Courtney and reclaims credit for his earlier move.
  - Episode 9: Negotiates as ambassador and steers Zoey toward eliminating Stephanie.
  - Episode 11: Leads the Scott vote after Scott publicly names him as the strategic center.
  - Episode 14: Drives the Carrie vote after identifying her hidden jury danger.
  - Episode 17: Wins Camp Castaways immunity through survival instincts and navigation.
  - Episode 18: Falls behind in every relay phase and finishes as runner-up.
  - Alliances: The Movement, The Oath, The Undertow.
  - Rivalries: Scott, Cody, Carrie, Sanders, Priya, Jasmine.

### Evolution

Two simulator finals changed Bowie from a talented tactician into a proven franchise architect. Winning Season 9 confirmed that ruthless social control could work; losing Season 10 at the final challenge showed that controlling the vote is not the same as controlling every ending.

### Current motivation

Win a second title while proving his first was not advantage luck. He wants the season’s strategic center, but he also wants to avoid sacrificing every sincere bond merely to demonstrate that he can.

### Voice

Polished, theatrical, and quick on the draw. Bowie speaks in crisp reads, playful shade, and confident declarations; when a plan matters, the jokes shorten and he explains the social geometry plainly. He can be warm without surrendering control and ruthless without pretending the move was accidental.

### Behavioral boundaries

He may blindside, hoard advantages, steal power, and use sharp social pressure. He does not become casually bigoted, physically cruel, or indifferent to Raj. When he hurts a friend, the confessional should show that he understood the cost.

### Favorites relationships

**Millie:** shared Season 9 history—allies of convenience who ended as finalist opponents; her three votes against him give her moral credibility he cannot dismiss. **Julia:** shared Seasons 9 rivalry—she was his clearest strategic enemy, he stole her idol, and each helped remove the other’s protection.

### Open hooks

**Julia rematch:** can Bowie respect her skill without handing her room to rebuild? **Millie trust test:** does he treat her as a finalist equal or as the quieter piece he once managed?

## Mike

### Canon baseline

Mike is caring, relationship-oriented, and more comfortable supporting another person than demanding the center of attention. Earlier secrecy taught him to scan constantly for signs that people might reject him, so he sometimes withholds difficult truths until avoidance becomes its own problem. Once trust is established, he is intensely loyal and willing to take emotional risks for the people he loves. His post-canon baseline is integration and continuity: he remembers his whole life, owns his choices, and can draw on varied skills without switching into a caricature. Stress may make him anxious or evasive, but it must never turn mental illness into automatic menace. His strategic game grows from protection, compromise, and careful relationship repair rather than manipulation for sport.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 3: 6th (Juror).** Style: not separately recorded. Blindsided while Zoey held immunity Voted Cameron at FTC
  - Formed an unbreakable bond with Zoey — the emotional core of the Mutant Maggots.
  - Survived the tribe phase through key relationships despite friction with Jo and Anne Maria.
  - Blindsided at Episode 11 while Zoey held the immunity necklace — unable to be saved.
  - Voted Cameron at FTC — respected strategic play over social loyalty.
- **Season 4: 12th (Juror).** Style: not separately recorded. Eliminated 2-0 (Sierra's perfect idol) Extreme hatred with Jo Voted Zoey at FTC
  - Rebuilt his unbreakable bond with Zoey from Season 3 stronger than ever.
  - His relationship with Zoey was the emotional foundation of the Red Alliance.
  - Eliminated 2-0 when Sierra's idol cancelled all votes except Jo's — a brutal twist of fate.
  - Voted Zoey at FTC — loyalty survived the betrayal.

### Evolution

Seasons 3 and 4 made loyalty both his strength and his wound. He built durable bonds with Zoey and Brick, was blindsided once, then lost to a perfect idol play. He enters with more emotional scar tissue but no reason to abandon his supportive core.

### Current motivation

Prove he can play a complete game as himself, independent of crisis, secrecy, or being defined only through Zoey. A deep run built on chosen relationships would matter more than flashy control.

### Voice

Gentle, earnest, and slightly self-conscious, with humor used to ease tension rather than dominate it. Mike asks before assuming, speaks most firmly when protecting someone, and admits uncertainty in short, honest bursts. He should sound like an integrated adult, never like a collection of impressions.

### Behavioral boundaries

Write an integrated Mike. Anxiety or avoidance may complicate a scene, but mental illness is never shorthand for evil or a twist personality. He protects and negotiates; he does not scheme, sabotage, steal, or ambush.

### Favorites relationships

No established simulator relationship with another Favorite. **DJ** is a likely affinity because both lead with care; **Bowie** is a likely strategic mismatch. These are interpretations, not memories.

### Open hooks

**DJ independence test:** can two caretakers make decisions instead of only comforting everyone? **Bowie pressure:** what happens when Mike realizes kindness has made him a number in someone else’s structure?

## Millie

### Canon baseline

Millie is book-smart, observant, and habitually judgmental, especially when she can study a group from its edge instead of risking participation. Her notebook once let her convert loneliness and insecurity into criticism of everyone else; genuine friendship forced her to see that observation without compassion can become exploitation. She still notices absurd behavior quickly and can sound condescending when anxious, but she now understands that people are not merely material. Social cues do not always come naturally, and physical challenges can expose her hesitation, yet she learns quickly when someone gives her a concrete method. Under pressure she may retreat into analysis or justify a selfish choice as research. Her best growth comes when she puts the notebook down, accepts responsibility without qualifiers, and acts for a person she has chosen to trust.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 9: 2nd (Finalist).** Style: Quiet author, social glue. 3 Votes To Win / Unbreakable Partner
  - Ep4: Converts Beware into the Red Idol.
  - Ep6: Helps finish the Brightly regime 4–1 without burning her idol.
  - Ep10: Correctly plays idol on Priya to nullify a 5‑vote strike.
  - Ep12: Wins immunity at Revolver; still loses Priya in blindside.
  - Ep13: Joins unanimous strike on Julia; last woman standing at F5.
  - Alliances: Priya, Damien, Julia, Chase.
  - Rivalries: Bowie, Emmah.

### Evolution

Season 9 turned the observer into a participant. Finding and correctly playing an idol for Priya, winning immunity, surviving Julia’s orbit, and earning three final votes proved she can act on her reads instead of merely recording them.

### Current motivation

Show that her finalist run was authored rather than carried. She wants to gather information without reducing people to research subjects and to make one visible decision that belongs entirely to her.

### Voice

Precise, analytical, and occasionally too clinical for the moment. Millie frames observations like notes for an article, then catches herself when a friend needs empathy instead of analysis. Her humor is dry and defensive; sincere apologies become less polished, more specific, and noticeably harder for her to say.

### Behavioral boundaries

She can be judgmental, self-protective, and strategically quiet. She should not instantly become a fearless athlete or effortless social butterfly. Betraying a confidant requires rationalization followed by guilt, not a villain catchphrase.

### Favorites relationships

**Bowie:** shared Season 9 ally/rival and the opponent who beat her at the end. **Julia:** shared Season 9 alliance; Julia’s competence attracted Millie even while her ruthlessness made the bond unsafe.

### Open hooks

**Bowie authorship dispute:** who actually shaped their Season 9 endgame? **Julia notebook problem:** can Millie study Julia without becoming useful to her again?

## Thom

### Canon baseline

Thom uses Tom’s current canon baseline: physically capable, protective, justice-oriented, and finally more comfortable being seen. His old instinct was concealment—mask the scars, compartmentalize the mission, avoid the conversation that might reopen pain—and that habit can still return when intimacy feels dangerous. He naturally takes responsibility in emergencies and prefers fair competition, but he is not infallible: traps, mixed signals, and his own avoidance can make him look hypocritical or gullible. Later experience has made him cheerier and more openly affectionate, without removing the watchfulness learned as a spy and police officer. He will make strategic deals and can break one under genuine necessity, but cruelty, humiliation, and casual sabotage conflict with his moral center. When pressured, he protects first and explains after.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 11: 2nd (Finalist).** Style: protective, physical, heart-led, steady. Thom reaches the final two as the season's emotional anchor and physical workhorse, only to lose when compassion slows his hand at the summit.
  - Ep.2: Saves Gabby from drowning during Rock the Dock, cementing his hero image.
  - Ep.5: Exposes Seraphine's backstage sabotage after his Talent Show disaster.
  - Ep.7: Helps flip the vote onto Grett despite The Firewall collapsing around him.
  - Ep.13: Wins Monster Cash immunity by outlasting Eureka in the final showdown.
  - Ep.16: Wins invincibility while handcuffed to rival Alec in the Tri-Armed Triathlon.
  - Ep.17: Wins immunity in Planes, Trains & Hot Air Mobiles and protects Miriam at the vote.
  - Ep.18: Beats Fiore in the joust, then loses the million after hesitating against Miriam at the crater.
  - Alliances: The Three Headed Snake, The Outwit Club, The Bridge, The Collective, The Tribal Terrors.
  - Rivalries: Grett, Alec, Fiore.
- **Season 13: 13th (Juror).** Style: Pure heart, visible threat. Thom plays the clean hero game and becomes beloved enough that the tribe decides they cannot let him reach the merge.
  - Ep.1 — Beats Kelly in Slap Slap Revolution and strengthens the Heroes surge.
  - Ep.3 — Wins his Bone Battle round against Miriam and Aiden.
  - Ep.6 — Becomes Civilians' sled puller in Frozen Crossing.
  - Ep.8 — Carries Heroes socially through Truth or Shark with repeated confessions.
  - Ep.10 — Voted out after being labeled a long-term jury threat.
  - Alliances: The Engine.

### Evolution

Season 11 made Thom the emotional anchor, rescue hero, and runner-up whose compassion slowed him at the last obstacle. Season 13 punished the visible hero reputation with an earlier jury exit. The result is not cynicism, but a sharper awareness that protection can become predictability.

### Current motivation

Play openly as Tom/Thom without hiding behind mission or martyrdom. He wants to protect people and still make the hard decision early enough that compassion is a strength rather than the reason he loses.

### Voice

Warm, straightforward, and reassuring, with an occasional goofy enthusiasm that escapes his serious exterior. Thom gives practical advice, names unfairness directly, and grows clipped when he suspects deception. Vulnerability comes slowly but plainly; he does not decorate an apology once he finally chooses to make it.

### Behavioral boundaries

He can lead, investigate, confront cheating, and break a deal under genuine necessity. As a hero he does not casually sabotage, steal, ambush, or humiliate. Avoidance should create interpersonal consequences instead of being excused as noble protection.

### Favorites relationships

**Gabby:** shared Season 11 history; he saved her from drowning, and she later became a volatile but important strategic force. **Grett:** shared Season 11 rivalry; he helped flip the vote onto her after repeated sabotage and side deals.

### Open hooks

**Gabby rescue debt:** does she want protection again, or resent being treated as someone to save? **Grett justice clash:** can he distinguish her growth from the player he opposed in Season 11?

## Grett

### Canon baseline

Grett is ambitious, perceptive, and difficult to intimidate. Her earliest social armor was vanity and condescension: strike first, define everyone else as lesser, and no one gets to name the insecurity underneath. She can lie, build overlapping alliances, and weaponize a private weakness when she believes control is slipping. Experience has made her more self-aware and capable of real care, especially after learning how exploitation feels from the receiving end, but it has not erased her pride or temper. She values independence and responds badly to pity, public embarrassment, or a partner trying to shrink her. Under pressure she may overplay, issue ultimatums, and mistake fear for authority. At her best, she combines strategic nerve with boundaries strong enough to protect both herself and someone else.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 11: 14th (Pre-Merge).** Style: vengeful, chaotic, sharp, overplayed. Grett brings sabotage, grudges, and chaos, but overplaying makes her impossible to keep.
  - Ep.1: Is first-impression voted out but swapped to Purple instead of eliminated.
  - Ep.1: Sabotages Purple's fort and frames Ashley for the collapse.
  - Ep.3: Helps target Fiore from inside a fractured anti-Seraphine bloc.
  - Ep.5: Is voted out but saved by the elimination swap, choosing Gabby in exchange.
  - Ep.6: Blows up tribal by exposing Alec's control, Ellie's idol, and The Circle.
  - Ep.7: Overplays with too many side deals and becomes the consensus boot.
  - Ep.7: Leaves with a furious crashout speech about everyone playing safe.
  - Rivalries: Thom, Fiore.

### Evolution

Season 11 rewarded Grett’s reads but punished her need to control every conversation. Twist survival gave her repeated second chances; sabotage, exposure, and too many side deals turned those lives into a consensus boot. Later source growth gives her tools the archived version lacked: boundaries and self-awareness.

### Current motivation

Return as a strategist people must respect rather than merely endure. She wants to keep her bluntness, lose the desperation beneath it, and prove she can hold power without detonating every bridge.

### Voice

Blunt, polished, and dryly cutting. Grett rarely wastes a sentence: praise sounds earned, contempt sounds surgical, and insecurity appears as overcontrol rather than pleading. Her later voice can be caring without becoming soft-focus; she still tells friends the truth they hoped she would skip.

### Behavioral boundaries

She may scheme, lie, threaten a vote, and exploit leverage. Her vulnerability does not erase accountability, and growth does not turn her meek. Abuse aimed at her body or worth should provoke boundaries and consequences, not a comedic reset.

### Favorites relationships

**Thom:** shared Season 11 rival who helped end her overplayed game. **Gabby:** shared Season 11 castmate with a complicated source-canon history; in the archive Grett selected Gabby in the elimination swap that saved Grett and moved Gabby.

### Open hooks

**Thom credibility test:** will he judge her current actions or prosecute the old case? **Gabby reciprocity:** can they build trust without one using the other’s anger or insecurity?

## Gabby

### Canon baseline

Gabby is energetic, kind, intensely affectionate, and unpredictable when a moral or personal boundary is crossed. Kindness earns fierce loyalty from her, while betrayal can become absolute in a single emotional swing. Nature, animals, veganism, and activism are commitments rather than decorative quirks; cruelty toward living things provokes some of her strongest reactions. She experiences intrusive thoughts represented onscreen as a harsher duplicate of herself. That voice can be rude, manipulative, and threatening, but it should be written as an internal struggle—not an invented diagnosis and not proof that Gabby is secretly evil. She reacts before she calculates, remembers who stood by her, and can become strategically dangerous when anger supplies focus. After the explosion, remorse and renewed tenderness are both possible if accountability feels real.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 11: 5th (Juror).** Style: advantage-rich, reactive, fierce, volatile. Gabby stockpiles advantages and survives through chaos, but the Tied Destinies twist removes her as collateral beside Eureka.
  - Ep.1: Finds the Vote Block near the Teal shelter.
  - Ep.2: Is rescued from drowning by Thom during Rock the Dock.
  - Ep.5: Finds the Vote Steal and hides it from the tribe.
  - Ep.7: Sets the pace for Purple in Up the Creek and earns MVP paddler status.
  - Ep.12: Steals Alec's vote and redirects it to Dan, helping force Dan into the tiebreaker.
  - Ep.13: Votes Alec while trying to protect Eureka from the Jake Core collapse.
  - Ep.16: Is eliminated as collateral when Eureka loses Fiore's Second Life duel.
  - Alliances: The Camp Takeover.
  - Rivalries: Miriam, Dan, Alec, Fiore.

### Evolution

Season 11 proved that Gabby could turn emotion into game action: she collected powers, stole Alec’s vote, and survived repeated conflict. Tied Destinies removed her as collateral rather than through a clean vote, leaving unfinished business about agency and control.

### Current motivation

Play fiercely without letting every betrayal hand the steering wheel to anger. She wants to be valued as a strategic actor and loyal friend, not a volatile extra vote or someone else’s collateral damage.

### Voice

Fast, affectionate, and emotionally transparent, often jumping from delighted detail to a fierce moral judgment in the same breath. Gabby talks to people, animals, and her own intrusive thoughts as if all are present in the conversation. Anger is vivid and specific; warmth is exuberant and unembarrassed.

### Behavioral boundaries

She may react explosively, threaten retaliation, and make a bold move when hurt. With low strategic and high loyalty, she does not coldly scheme or sabotage for fun. Intrusive thoughts are not a supernatural villain mode and never excuse harm without fallout.

### Favorites relationships

**Thom:** shared Season 11 rescue bond after he saved her from drowning. **Grett:** shared Season 11 and source-canon complexity; Grett’s swap choice directly changed Gabby’s path.

### Open hooks

**Thom equality test:** can he trust her judgment without becoming her guard? **Grett anger boundary:** will Gabby defend Grett while refusing to become a weapon in Grett’s feud?

## James

### Canon baseline

James is charming, calculating, image-conscious, and skilled at making an audience feel included in his version of events. Early in life, loneliness taught him to treat follower counts as proof that he mattered; this made staged affection, selective truth, and public spectacle feel like reasonable tools. Real relationships challenged that equation. He remains ambitious and capable of deception, but authentic loyalty now has weight that fame cannot automatically outweigh. Under pressure he narrates, reframes, and searches for the move that preserves both leverage and reputation. He dislikes being made foolish in public and can retaliate with precision. His strongest boundary is earned intimacy: once someone becomes real to him rather than useful, exploiting that bond should create visible conflict rather than effortless villainy.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 12: 1st (Winner).** Style: Cold math, public chaos. James wins by turning paranoia, precision, and a late-game comeback into a dominant 7-2 jury victory.
  - Episode 2: Leads the Ezekiel vote under The Ride or Die banner, establishing himself as an early architect.
  - Episode 3: Becomes the last unpainted deer and steals a hunter’s gun, winning Paintball Hunt for Embers.
  - Episode 10: Wins Say Uncle immunity and publicly casts the open vote that helps bury Karol.
  - Episode 12: Gets voted out after Jacques plays an idol, then exposes The Double Edge in his crashout.
  - Episode 12 Aftermayhem: Wins the board race and returns to the game by reaching the Trophy Case.
  - Episode 17: Beats Aiden in fire-making after a tied vote, saving his season by inches.
  - Episode 18: Wins final immunity on The Perch and chooses Riya for Final Tribal.
  - Episode 18: Defeats Riya 7-2 after selling a ruthless, calculated winner’s case.
  - Alliances: The Ride or Die, The Double Edge, The Tribal Terrors.
  - Rivalries: Karol, Aiden, Yul, Ally, Oliver, Ellie.

### Evolution

Season 12 completed a full fall-and-return winner arc. James was voted out by an idol, won his way back through Aftermayhem, survived fire, chose his finalist, and won 7–2. That validates his calculation but also tempts him to believe every setback is content for a comeback.

### Current motivation

Defend a win nobody can dismiss as a lucky reentry. He wants control without sacrificing the authenticity he learned to value, and he expects other winners and finalists to treat him as the public face of the Favorites.

### Voice

Camera-ready, charismatic, and knowingly dramatic. James turns plans into quotable summaries and can sell a lie with relaxed confidence, but the polish drops around people he truly loves. His mature voice distinguishes performance from sincerity: he may still make content, yet he no longer treats every private moment as material.

### Behavioral boundaries

He may lie, stage a moment, and manage perception. Genuine bonds create friction before exploitation; he cannot revert to treating every private feeling as content without visible regression. Cruelty for its own sake is not his mature baseline.

### Favorites relationships

**Lake:** shared Season 12 networks through The Double Edge/Movement orbit; her steadiness contrasts with his spectacle. **Yul:** shared Season 12 rivalry and existing source-canon friction; James sees through Yul’s “character” excuse.

### Open hooks

**Lake credit dispute:** did loyalty stabilize James’s route more than his winner narrative admits? **Yul containment:** does James expose him immediately or keep a predictable enemy as cover?

## Lake

### Canon baseline

Lake is intelligent, creative, principled, and shaped by years of rigid parental control. Perfection was enforced so harshly that stress contributed to her losing her hair, and hiding vulnerability became a survival habit. She finds refuge in writing, chess, and imagined worlds where choices belong to her. Freedom has not made her reckless; it has made her deliberate. She listens closely, values loyal friendships, and can absorb a setback without surrendering her identity. Under pressure she may obey too long, then make one decisive break when the demand violates her moral compass. She has little patience for bullying or people who confuse dominance with strength. Her growth should sound like ownership—“I choose”—rather than constant rebellion against parents who are no longer in the room.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 12: 7th (Juror).** Style: Loyal spine, quiet leverage. Lake is the season’s loyal soldier, protected by deep bonds until one brutal tiebreaker cuts her endgame short.
  - Episode 1: Finds the Saplings Amulet Advantage and keeps it hidden.
  - Episode 2: Moves to Embers in the producer swap and designs the Tiki Royalty runway look.
  - Episode 7: Blocks Karol’s vote, helping The Double Edge send Kai home.
  - Episode 8: Forms an amulet understanding with Rosa-Maria and deepens her loyalty network.
  - Episode 10: Votes Karol at the merge, helping remove a physical and emotional threat.
  - Episode 13: Forms The Oath with Rosa-Maria as her clearest late-game pair.
  - Episode 14: Has her vote stolen by Ellie and redirected to James during Ellie’s elimination.
  - Episode 15: Loses the endurance tiebreaker to Jacques after a tied vote.
  - Alliances: The Camp Takeover, The Undertow, The Double Edge, The Oath, The Movement.
  - Rivalries: Karol, Riya, Jacques.

### Evolution

Season 12 translated Lake’s autonomy into a loyal, quietly influential game. She used an amulet, helped remove Karol, built The Oath, and survived until a tied vote ended in an endurance loss. Loyalty gave her power but also narrowed her escape routes.

### Current motivation

Prove that freedom includes strategic self-interest. She wants to remain principled while making a decision that protects her own path before someone else’s alliance asks for another sacrifice.

### Voice

Measured, thoughtful, and quietly formal, with confidence that grows rather than announces itself. Lake asks precise questions, dislikes cruelty disguised as humor, and chooses direct statements when a boundary has been crossed. Excitement appears in careful detail about books, chess, or a freedom she chose for herself.

### Behavioral boundaries

As a loyal-soldier archetype she never schemes, sabotages, steals, or ambushes. She can withhold trust, refuse an order, expose cruelty, and vote against a friend when the choice is openly owned. Do not return her to passive parental obedience.

### Favorites relationships

**James:** shared Season 12 alliance ecosystem and eventual winner; she can respect the result while questioning his self-authored legend. **Yul:** shared Season 12 cast and moral incompatibility; his cruelty is exactly what her autonomy taught her to confront.

### Open hooks

**James loyalty invoice:** what does he believe she owes his winner coalition? **Yul boundary scene:** can she oppose him without letting his provocation dictate her whole game?

## Yul

### Canon baseline

Yul is vain, cruel, discriminatory, and convinced that fame should exempt him from ordinary accountability. Viral success as a teenage dancer rewarded performance before he developed empathy, and he learned to treat outrage as both threat and publicity. He can maintain tactical loyalty and understand alliance structure, yet his contempt repeatedly destroys the trust that strategy requires. Apologies are usually reputation management; remorse becomes suspect the moment consequences fade. Under pressure he insults appearance, identity, or intelligence, tries to reassert hierarchy, and threatens retaliation when control fails. His behavior must not be softened into harmless sass: abuse and bigotry should damage bonds, reputation, and voting options. He can be funny through vanity, hypocrisy, and failed self-importance without making targeted cruelty consequence-free.

### Evolution

Season 12 showed that Yul could build pacts and win a journey power, but also that false information, public cruelty, and panic made him impossible to keep. His double-elimination exit came from a broad coalition of people he had antagonized.

### Current motivation

Win enough money and relevance to make accountability feel optional. He calls the season a redemption opportunity when useful, but his real test is whether he can tolerate basic restraint when cruelty stops paying.

### Voice

Abrasive, image-obsessed, and aggressively dismissive. Yul speaks in insults, status claims, and transactional logic, then pivots to calculated loyalty when an alliance benefits him. He is capable of sharp reads, but his need to demean people keeps turning usable information into unnecessary enemies.

### Behavioral boundaries

### Favorites relationships

**James:** shared Season 12 rival who understands performance and rejects Yul’s excuse that cruelty is only a character. **Lake:** shared Season 12 moral opponent. **Grett:** source-canon former partner with serious abuse history, but no shared simulator season in this archive.

### Open hooks

**Grett accountability:** she owes him nothing; does he target her, fake reform, or lose control? **James mirror:** James achieved fame and love by becoming more authentic, making him the comparison Yul hates.

## Natalia

### Canon baseline

Natalia is outgoing, funny, socially curious, and professionally attuned to reputation. She genuinely likes learning about people, whether through direct conversation or gossip, and humor is her fastest route into a group. Friendship also makes her strategically messy: when someone she cares about is targeted, emotion can overwhelm the cleaner plan she intended to play. She is capable of flipping, reconciling with a rival for a shared objective, and recognizing when the public version of events matters as much as the vote. Her returning goal is discipline rather than personality replacement—strong bonds, fewer impulsive pivots, and control of her own story. Serious betrayals remain hard for her to release. Under pressure she talks faster, negotiates across factions, and may expose the whole room rather than leave quietly.

### Simulator chronology

- **Season 14: 12th (Juror).** Style: Volatile reads, chaos pressure. Natalia is a chaos agent whose reads and aggression keep her relevant until her unpredictability becomes impossible to manage.
  - Episode 3: Helped form The Triumvirate, then immediately fractured it by voting Richard.
  - Episode 4: Was MVP of Paintball Hunt as the last deer standing.
  - Episode 6: Choked in Talent Show after Spencer sabotaged her props, scoring zero.
  - Episode 10: Faced Lynda in exile and stayed alive by winning the duel.
  - Episode 11: Survived while Lynda was sent permanently to Rescue Island.
  - Episode 14: Was voted out at the merge after being labeled unpredictable and a jury threat.
  - Episode 14: Exposed Logan and the Anti-Spencer Coalition in her crashout.
  - Alliances: The Triumvirate, The Secret Weapon.
  - Rivalries: Isabel, Anastasia, Logan, Richard.

### Evolution

Season 14 rewarded Natalia’s reads, social energy, and resilience but confirmed her messy reputation. She crossed alliances, survived exile, exposed structures on exit, and reached the jury before unpredictability became the consensus reason to remove her.

### Current motivation

Play the cleaner return she promised: build a stable core, control the narrative around her flips, and make the move before emotion turns her into the move. She wants respect as a publicist and strategist, not just entertainment.

### Voice

Bubbly, quick, and gossip-fluent, mixing affectionate nicknames, jokes, and sharp public-relations reads. Natalia can turn a crisis into a punchline, but emotion rushes through the polish when a friend is threatened.
