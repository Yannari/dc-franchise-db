quote one-liners). The French auto-captions of full episodes posted to YouTube

## 1. The launch night, as it really runs (S14, 2 h 30)

1. **Cold open, the Voice.** "Ici la Voix" over a montage of last season: the
   winner, the best moments. Then the season's idea in one breath (S14: "this
   season more than ever, beware of appearances" — the year of fake realities).
2. **Teaser of the cast in their own words**, three-second clips, each a type:
   the strategist, the investigator ("I'll dig the worms out of their noses"),
   the liar ("lying, betraying, manipulating — it's the game I love most"), the
   observer.
3. **The host's opening** to the studio crowd, warm and jokey; then he turns to
   the big screen and BANTERS WITH THE VOICE ("Good evening, Voice." — "Good
   evening, Christophe. The Voice has an eye, ears, and a great many plans.").
4. **Each arrival is a little episode of its own** (about ten minutes each):
   - the housemate walks onto the set; the host keeps them a moment;
   - the Voice speaks to them; often a PRIVILEGE WITH A PRICE ("you are the
     first to enter, and that is a privilege; but every privilege comes with its
     price");
   - **the studio audience holds up signs that reveal the secret to the public**
     (the house never hears it);
   - the PORTRAIT film, in their own words: "people think I'm cold, but…", name,
     age, job, town, family ("my parents are my favourite humans on earth"),
     their love life, how they will play ("lying, manipulating, I'll do it with
     a happy heart — respectfully, of course"), a promise to the Voice ("I
     promise to be a great player, and I'll win the 100,000"), and the moment
     they learnt they were cast (crying with their family);
   - **the airlock**: the host checks on them through an earpiece ("how are you
     feeling?", "what's your biggest asset?"), then the Voice;
   - **a first mission** for some: hide the sign with your own secret before the
     next housemate walks in; get a code built from another housemate's shoe
     size and the number of eggs in the pantry; a group sent to a second house;
     a secret TURNED INTO A ROLE ("you thought you'd come in with a personal
     secret, but the Voice has seen your talent for… do you accept?" — "I
     accept." — "Your new secret is: I am the oracle of the house").
5. **Between arrivals: live in the house.** The first ones in tour the rooms
   ("it's a madness", the wall of clues, "three bedrooms!"), claim beds
   STRATEGICALLY ("I took the middle one"), introduce themselves ("Malo, with an
   L and an O"), explain the show to the newcomer who never watched it, hide to
   surprise the next arrival.
6. **"The list of secrets"**, in batches of two: the Voice tells a secret as a
   short story ("one summer, at his grandparents', when he was eight…") that
   ends "our housemate has for secret: …", WITHOUT saying whose. The host recaps
   the growing list with a joke for each.
7. Ad-break bumpers that re-state where everyone is and what is at stake.
8. Ends with the house complete, the Voice's first address and the rules.

**What the simulator lacks today:** the Voice's cold open over last season; the
cast teaser; the host and the Voice talking to each other; the portrait in
their own words (age, job, family, how they'll play, the promise); the secret
shown to the PUBLIC (signs); the airlock check-in; first missions and the
secret-to-role offer; live check-ins on the house between arrivals (the tour,
beds, introductions); the list of secrets told as stories.

## 2. A daily (la quotidienne, 52 min)

The order of a typical daily (S14 Q06, the first nominations week):

1. "Previously in Secret Story" (narrator), then the Voice's slogan.
2. **The nominees wake up.** A confessional each: "this morning when I woke up
   I wasn't okay. I kept thinking I'm nominated." Their friends rally them
   ("we're all staying together, I believe it — you have to believe it hard
   enough").
3. **A twist plays out** (S14: an investigator in the second house names a
   profile; the airlock door opens or it does not; the two meet; "it's the
   game, no hard feelings, brother").
4. **The hunt as conversation:**
   - a holder ENJOYING the wrong trail: "it amuses me, because it moves them
     away. I try to make it last — if they buzz it, it's over";
   - an investigator calling two people in: "why did I call you both? Because I
     have questions";
   - a theory about a hidden pair: "I'm convinced there's a couple, or siblings,
     who came in pretending not to know each other";
   - the holder's fear in the confessional while it happens ("he scares me on
     this one");
   - two housemates agreeing that the house guesses secrets from LOOKS ("they
     give us secrets from our bodies, it's all they have").
5. **A Voice game in the evening** (S14: the "Oops" night: a statement is read
   — "one of you thinks somebody has an accomplice", "one of you finds someone
   fake", "one of you has a crush" — and whoever it fits raises a sign). It
   sets off accusations said to the face, crushes admitted in front of
   everybody, a confession a housemate had been saving to mislead the hunters.
6. **A secret mission's progress** (the oracle finally finds the lock her key
   opens).
7. **Late at night: the alliance "core"** ("I want you in my core", "you're
   vicious" — "it's a game, there's 100,000 at the end"), a flirt that turns
   real, somebody finding a hidden door.
8. **The Voice closes the day**: the nominees named, the vote numbers.

Also seen in the dailies and the week compilation: the MINI-SECRETS game (the
Voice tells a small true anecdote about somebody; first to buzz the right name
wins $200, loses $100), a PRACTICE BUZZER (test a guess without the $5,000), the
BLACK BUZZER (whoever holds it is immune and nominates the other), a mission to
get somebody to buzz a FAKE secret, "settling-scores" dinners, the clue room,
nicknames for groups, songs and dances to pass the time.

## 3. A prime (the weekly live show; the semi-final read in full)

1. **The Voice addresses the house** ("tonight is your last chance").
2. **A narrated package of the week**: clips with a voice-over joining the
   week's storylines (the race for a place in the final, a betrayal, a
   reconciliation attempt, who allied with whom), quoting the housemates.
3. **The host opens, with LAST WEEK'S ELIMINATED on the studio couch** (who
   stays all night, reacts, is asked questions; his hair dyed by fans' vote).
4. **Live calls to the house, room by room** ("two rooms, two moods"): the
   finalists first ("are you proud you kept your secret to the last buzz?",
   "how's the mood in there?"), then the nominees ("how are you feeling about
   tonight?" — "stressed"); the host states the stakes and the vote numbers.
5. **The kitty board** revealed to the public, then to the house (who reacts
   out loud: "29,000!").
6. **Dilemmas where your choice costs someone else**: swap kitties with the
   richest, but your friend's cousin's visit is cancelled; the RED PHONE
   answerer lets a fiancée see her partner in the airlock while another
   housemate's kitty drops $100 a second, and the house watches the reunion
   while the money runs; a "liar's poker" of two sealed cards (steal / share)
   where one housemate must decide who is lying.
7. **A comic mission played live** (a fake old dance teacher arrives with his
   whole troupe; the finalist must pretend to remember him).
8. **The Golden Buzz**: a blooper reel of the week's buzz attempts and wild
   theories, with joke awards.
9. **The verdict in stages**: votes paused, a bailiff brings the envelope, the
   nominees in the airlock, the host's speech ("you have known highs and lows,
   you have seen friends leave… the public has decided"), names called one by
   one, the saved go back in.
10. **The eliminated comes up to the set**: greets family in the audience, is
    interviewed (regrets, "what would you tell the viewers?"), and is given a
    FAREWELL POWER (hand out ÷2, a kitty switch, +$2,000 and +$5,000 to the
    nominees still in).
11. Ad bumpers restate the vote numbers and the stakes every segment.

## 4. A final

The finalists' last day; ALL the eliminated back in the studio; each finalist's
journey film and the same questions ("what would you do with the 100,000?",
regrets, apologies); joke awards and a reel of the season's 312 theories; THE
VOICE'S FAREWELL to each finalist, personal ("you're a real Marseillaise… your
secret was a hard one to keep… it was your soulmate in here who found it");
the finalists finally see the eliminated; the bailiff's envelope; last place to
first; the winner's speech.

## 5. How they talk

- **The Voice**: formal, warm, a little sly. Always "Ici la Voix" ("This is the
  Voice"), always "C'est tout pour le moment" ("That is all for now"), the
  housemate's name first, the Voice in the third person ("the Voice has
  decided", "the Voice offers you"), short orders ("please come to the
  confessional immediately"), "Décision validée" ("Decision confirmed").
- **The host**: warm, jokey, explains everything twice, recaps the stakes, says
  the vote numbers every segment, teases what is coming ("in a moment…"),
  talks to the housemates through their earpieces.
- **The housemates**: spoken, young, emotional, a lot of repetition and
  exclamation ("it's a madness", "I can't believe it"), quick oaths ("I swear"),
  "it's the game", "no hard feelings", "for 100,000", the alliance as a family
  ("my core", "my pillars", "my brother"), confessionals that name the feeling
  and the reason in one breath ("I'm gutted, because…").
- English for the simulator (the user's rule): the same register in natural
  English. No translated idioms; no quips; every scene names its subject
  (`feedback_bb_no_clever_lines`).

## 6. Part 3 — what to build, in order

   little stories.

   the week from its own aired scenes, last week's eliminated on the couch,
   live calls room by room, the kitty board live, dilemmas that cost somebody
   else, the visit with a running price, the Golden Buzz reel, the verdict in
   stages with the bailiff, the eliminated's interview and FAREWELL POWER.

   director stops airing Big Brother's strategy steps (HOH pitches, veto,

   the hunt as conversation (theories, interrogations, the holder's fear and
   delight, the hidden-pair theory), the kitty, the nominees' morning and the
   rally, nominations talk (forbidden, so whispered), the public, missions
   acted out, the alliance core, settling scores.
4. **The Voice's evening games** (the "Oops" night, mini-secrets) and the black
   buzzer, as recurring tools.
5. **The final as the real one**: the eliminated in the studio, journeys with
   the same questions, joke awards, the Voice's farewell to each finalist.
