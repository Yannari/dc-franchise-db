# Total Drama / Disventure Camp dialogue — what the real episodes do

Read from the transcripts measured in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-td-storylines-design.md` §1b
(TDI on totaldramaislandtranscript.wordpress.com; DC4/DC5 on disventurecamp.fandom.com, `<Episode>/Transcript`).
Every TD story pool is written against this. Never copy a line from a transcript; copy what it does.

## 1. Continuity is the whole show

Real camp talk names what happened earlier, by name, all the time:

- "Thank you for convincing me about Spencer." / "...make up for the Natalia vote." / "Not since the pirate
  challenge." / "Is this about the shower?" / "Remember him and Diego?"
- A plan names its mechanics and its numbers: "Misdirect Anastasia and Marissa to split the vote, Amelie and
  Hannah side with us, and boom. We're there."
- The losing team blames somebody specific, for something specific: "He lost us the challenge; plus, he's
  been throwing out my ally's name."

So a story line has a `{slot}` for the thing that happened (`{lastBoot}`, `{myVote}`, `{sank}`, `{carried}`,
`{alliance}`, `{target}`), and its `when` asks for the fact that makes it true. A line with no fact behind
it is texture and belongs in a quick cut.

## 2. Nobody agrees in two lines

- "Without me?" / "I made an executive decision." / "OK, but I wanna side with Anastasia." The pitch gets
  questioned, the plan gets counter-planned.
- People ask why: "Why him?" "Why tell me?" "And why?" "Does there have to be a reason?"
- Somebody walks in, cuts in or overhears ("So… Isabel and Jade, huh?"). A third person turns a two-person scene.

## 3. Something is always going on underneath

Strategy happens while they're doing something: gutting fish, hunting for bullets, rowing a boat, stretching
on the dock, braiding hair, writing in a journal. In TDI most camp scenes are about the episode's challenge
(the talent show auditions, the cook-off, the hike), so the morning is preparation and the evening is fallout.

## 4. Characters sound like themselves

- Insults and comebacks in plain words: "Beat it." "Too late." "Pfft. And? You're not my mom." "Get bent."
- The sweet one babbles and overshares (Lindsay's sister story). The schemer is polite until she isn't
  ("Oh, and easy on the chips"). The cool one says one dry line and leaves ("Anywhere that's not here.").
- An emotional turn comes out of a real wound and is said raw, not tidily: "Who cares? She's dead!"
- Plain spoken English, contractions, trailing off ("I… wouldn't be so sure."), interruptions marked with a
  dash. No therapy-speak, no epigrams, no narrator cleverness.

## 5. Confessionals are motive, said plainly

One sharp thought, often the thing they would never say out loud in camp:
"Amelie taking Isabel's side? Over mine?" / "Knowing Anastasia and being burned by her before, her trust can
easily just go… poof! I can't take the chance." / "What a bunch of losers. It's so easy, it's almost not fun…
almost." A confessional can contradict what the speaker just said in the scene. That is the point of it.

## 6. The vote and the elimination

- **Voting booth (DC):** each voter gives their reason in their own voice, with attitude: "Looks like it's
  time for you to head back to the convent." / "I hate to do this to a teammate, but I need to keep the heat
  off myself." Show every voter (the user's rule); the reason is the engine's.
- **The reading:** blindside reactions and a confrontation right there ("You know what you did." / "What the
  hell are you talking about?!" / "Hope you can respect a game move.").
- **The host's bottom two (TDI):** Chris says why each one is in danger, from what they did this episode.
- **Last words / the Dock of Shame:** a goodbye with the boot's closest person, or a parting shot at the
  person who did it ("You are going to hear from my attorney."), sometimes a gift.
- **After:** one or two confessionals from the people who did it, or from the boot's friend swearing revenge
  ("If that evil little cow thinks she is getting away with this…").

## 7. Lengths

TD camp scenes run median 4 spoken lines (p90 13); DC median 8 (p90 18). The long scenes are the turning
points: a betrayal confronted, a plan made, an alliance cracking. A quick cut of 1–3 lines between them is
normal and good.
