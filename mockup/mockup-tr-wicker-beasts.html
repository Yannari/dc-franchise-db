Wicker Beasts

The Briefing

Alan Cumming · your host

A riverside field. Two giant beasts woven from willow stand at the far end, a stag and a boar. Heaps of storm debris line the near bank, and two rowing boats are tied up at the water.

Alan Cumming stands between the two beasts, one hand resting on the stag's woven foreleg.

Each team has a beast. Your job is to burn it.

To burn it you need a fuse and a flame. The rope for the fuse is buried in that debris. Half of you dig it out and tie it into one long line from the beast to the bank.

Alan Cumming points across the river to a single torch burning on a post.

The other half row across and bring back the flame. Let it go out and you row back for another. Tie a bad knot and the fire stops at it.

Somewhere in that debris is a small wicker hare, and inside the hare is a Shield. Whoever opens it keeps it.

Whoever stops to open it is not finding rope, and your fuse will be shorter for it.

The first beast to burn is ten thousand in the pot. The second is five. A beast that is still standing in thirty minutes earns nothing. Go.

Alan Cumming steps well back from both beasts.
