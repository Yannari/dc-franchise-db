Mission · Episode Four

The Drowned Causeway

Eighteen players, ten strongboxes, four hundred yards of wet sand and about seventy minutes before the sea closes the road behind them.

Three phases Gulls v Herons £2,000 a box No shield

The Briefing

The landward end of a tidal causeway at half-tide. A painted tide board on a post, a line of sealed strongboxes on the shingle, and a ruined chapel about four hundred yards out on the sand.

The host stands at the landward end of the causeway with the tide board behind them. The sea is already visible at both edges of the sand.

Out there is a chapel. It has been cut off twice a day for four hundred years, and in about seventy minutes it will be cut off again.

The host turns and points along the wet road to the chapel.

Between here and it are ten sealed strongboxes, half of them marked for one team and half for the other. You will carry them out along the causeway. That is the easy part, and I would enjoy it.

A crew member lifts the corner of a tarpaulin at the chapel wall, showing a stone ledge about a foot wide running above the channel.

You cannot take a box in through the chapel door, because by the time you reach it there will be water across the threshold. You take it along that ledge, hand to hand, and up onto the roof.

If a box goes into the channel, it is gone. Nobody goes in after it. If you drop a box on the sand you may go back for it, and you will lose the time it takes you, and the tide does not wait while you decide.

The host walks to the foot of the tower and puts a hand on the bell rope.

When your boxes are up, one of you rings this bell. The tide board will tell you how many times — read it properly, because a wrong count opens the wrong hatch, and the wrong hatch drops a box straight back onto the sand.

Every strongbox on that roof when I stop the clock is two thousand into the pot you are all playing for. Every box still down here earns you nothing except the walk back.

The host steps aside and looks at the water.

The road closes when it closes. Whoever is on the wrong side of it when that happens finishes the afternoon watching. Go.
