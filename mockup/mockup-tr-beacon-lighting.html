Beacon Lighting

Every one of you starts tied to a pole in the loch. Forty minutes to get free, build the beacon from the plans in the bottles, get out of the water, and set it on fire.

The Briefing

Alan Cumming · your host

A loch at dusk. Twelve wooden poles standing in the shallows, a raft moored in the middle with an empty iron frame on it, and a line of bottles along the shingle.

Alan Cumming stands on the shingle with a lit torch, and the players are waist-deep in the loch, each one tied to a pole.

You are tied on, and you will have to get yourselves off. Nobody on the bank will help you.

Out on that raft is a frame. The pieces that fill it are in the water around you, and the plan for how they go together is in the bottles along this shore. Put every piece in the right place.

Alan Cumming lowers the torch toward the water and stops.

The beacon will not light while there is anybody in the loch. Every one of you has to be on dry land before I put this to it.

There are three Shields. Two are out in the water. One is somewhere on this shore.

Whoever goes for one is not untying anybody, not carrying pieces, and, if it is in the water, not out of it.

If the beacon burns inside forty minutes, fifteen thousand goes into the pot. If it does not, nothing does. Start.
