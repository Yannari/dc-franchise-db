The Den, and the night it turns red

Back to the BB19 room, done properly. The problem with the first pass was not the direction — it was that red was wallpaper. Everything was red all the time, so red meant nothing.

There are 5 apples, and before nominations, a HouseGuest can pick one… when the lights on the Tree turn red. If an apple is chosen, they will either get a prize or a punishment. Big Brother 19 · Tree of Temptation

Read that line again: the lights turn red when an offer is live. Red is the show's signal, not its decor. So the Den at rest is mahogany, gilt and candlelight — and red is what arrives when the season turns on the house.

The gilt frames, the tufted velvet wall, the lamp glow from below and the deep vignette are the furniture the first pass never had. The middle column is the room you sit in for five weeks. The right-hand one is the same room with the lights up.

The two right-hand columns are the same theme. Nothing in the arc, the schedule or the voice changed between them — only mood, which already flips at week 6 and until now did nothing but swap a word pool. Because the Den is warm for five weeks, the night it goes red actually lands.

Scope, honestly

Built once, used by every theme

The token layer is the real work: bbns, bbvc, bbev, bbct, bbop currently hard-code their colours. Tokenising them is mechanical — and afterwards BBAI or the Multiverse is a palette and a handful of rules.

Mood becomes a visual system

setThemeMood('hostile') already fires at week 6 and already survives into saved seasons. Today it swaps a line pool. Putting it on the reader root as a class is a few lines, and every theme with a heel turn inherits it — AINSLEY's week-10 turn gets this for free.

Needs more than CSS

Two hooks, not three: the SVG on an unrevealed screen, and a per-mood room wash. Keeping the wall called Memory Wall means no vocab hook is needed for this theme — BB19 never renamed it either.

Still not a bespoke set

This dresses shared screens beautifully. It does not build Temptation its own ceremony the way Get a Clue has one — no Den you actually walk into, no door in the wall. That is a further piece, worth doing and worth scoping on its own.

Decisions I need: does the Den read rich enough now, and do you want the token layer first (every theme after this gets cheap) or Temptation's own bespoke ceremony first (only this theme benefits)? One loose end either way — the antagonist is still called The Den, which is also the id of the twist the arc books. Same word, two things.
