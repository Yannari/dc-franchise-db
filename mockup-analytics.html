Week 2 — the verdict
Nico won the power. Ireland spent it.

Nico took the Head of Household in a thirteen-minute endurance hold and never ran the week. Ireland held the Interrogation, took the ceremony out of his hands, and moved the block onto Zella — a member of the alliance she is aligned with — without a single houseguest knowing a power had been played.

The eviction itself was never close. Six of the ten votes against Stella never had to be moved, and the majority was seven, so the week's real work was one conversation, not a campaign. What looks like a coalition decision was a near-consensus that Ireland only had to nudge.

Decision points

Judged on what was knowable at the time, not on the result
01
Before nominations

Ireland plays the Interrogation and names Joel

She could have stayed quiet and kept the power for a week where she was actually in danger. Instead she spent it while safe, to move a block that was not aimed at her.

Chose

Named Joel as a decoy Head of Household and redirected the ceremony.

Alternative

Bank it. She was not a target this week and had no reason to be seen doing anything.

Verdict

Correct, narrowly. The power expires, Amberly was going up, and nobody traced it. She bought a week for free.

02
Veto ceremony

Zella goes up as the replacement

The renom hit a member of the alliance Ireland is aligned with — the one decision this week that created a permanent enemy.

Chose

Zella, a Power Couple vote, over any of the four unaligned houseguests.

Alternative

Aaron. Nobody defends him, the week stays quiet, and the alliance never learns it is expendable.

Verdict

An error, and the season's first real one. It bought nothing — Zella won her way off — and cost the trust the whole plan runs on.

03
Eviction night

Harriett writes down her own alliance member

The Pipeline was two days old. She had the numbers to keep Stella and chose not to use them.

Chose

Voted Jules, breaking the deal she made, and left the room before it was counted.

Alternative

Vote with the Pipeline. It loses 9–4 anyway, and she keeps a partner and her word.

Verdict

A cost with no benefit. The vote was never in doubt — she paid full price for a result she was going to get for free.

After week 2 — the state of the game
Two blocs, one broker, and nobody has noticed her.

The Power Couple and the Plot Twist have seven votes between them and have not yet worked out that they want the same six people gone. Ireland sits in the second and has been quietly deciding for both.

The house has no idea a power was played, which means the season's most important fact is one nobody is looking for.
